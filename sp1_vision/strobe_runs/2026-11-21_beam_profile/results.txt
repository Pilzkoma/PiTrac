2026-11-21 IR beam profile on a wall (beam_profile.py)

Setup: unit square to a dark green wall ~48 cm away, white skirting board
along the bottom (up to ~-1.5 deg), floor below. Both arrays fire together
(wired in parallel); they are identical and parallel, so this is one
array's profile. Frames in JetsonLM_recordings/2026-11-21_beam_profile/.

Run 1, 100 us, whole band: meaningless - it measured the white skirting
against the green wall (scene.jpg), and the skirting clipped (rise 148/150).
Run 2, 300 us, --min-deg 1 (green wall only, +1.5 to +22.5 deg): wall rise
up to 61/63 grey levels, in the linear range.

Vertical profile along the image centre (relative to +1.5 deg, wall
geometry divided out, beam x lens fall-off): half light at +16.9 deg (cam1)
/ +15.2 deg (cam2); at the image top (+21 deg) 0.35. Both cameras agree.
Sideways (light_map_upper_wall.jpg, relative to the axis):
  centre column     +2 / +10 / +16 / +21 deg: 1.00 / 0.65 / 0.52 / 0.37
  20 deg sideways   ~0.4-0.8 / 0.3-0.6 / 0.3-0.45 / 0.25-0.36
  30 deg sideways (corners) 0.2-0.5 / 0.2-0.4 / 0.2-0.35 / 0.17-0.32
The beam is much wider than the earlier "halves ~9 deg above the axis"
(read off the chips' fading residual, 2026-11-12) - that was not a beam
measurement.

Below the axis nothing was measured on one surface; tilt options are
evaluated with the profile mirrored about its peak (assumption):
  tilts  0/ 0: rest ball (-13) 0.50, axis 0.92, +10 0.63, +16 0.50, +21 0.35
  tilts 10/ 0: rest ball 0.41, axis 0.74, +10 0.77, +16 0.64, +21 0.48
  tilts 16/-9: rest ball 0.53, axis 0.54, +10 0.54, +16 0.62, +21 0.58
                (best worst-point over -13..+22.5; leans on the mirror)
At 100 us the resting ball (-13 deg) gives +73 grey levels; at +21 deg the
ball would get ~0.7 of that by this profile (+50), and the 2026-11-13 pulse
test found +17 enough to see a ball. Brightness at the image top is not the
bottleneck for chips; a tilt buys ~+35-65 % up there at a cost lower down.

Tool bug found and fixed on the way: outside the measured range the
profile was mirrored and then clipped, which handed out the PEAK value
(a jump in the first suggested-tilt curve); now the darker end value.
